DATA SOURCES — Tides: NOAA CO-OPS station predictions (api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov), pulled live and matched to the nearest active station for each spot. Where a subordinate station publishes only high/low times, the curve between them is reconstructed by harmonic interpolation and marked as such. Weather: Open-Meteo forecast API (open-meteo.com). Waves, swell, and sea-surface temperature: Open-Meteo Marine API. Moon phase, moonrise/moonset, and solunar windows are computed in your browser from an abridged lunar theory (the main periodic terms in longitude and latitude), with rise and set refined by interpolation and corrected for horizontal parallax and refraction — expect a few minutes of error, not navigation-grade. All times are US Central, regardless of where you are reading this. The 0–100 fishability score is a heuristic combining all of the above; it is an estimate to help you compare spots and days, not a guarantee. Water temperature is shown for reference and is not part of the score.

How the score works

Each spot gets a 0–100 score built from five live-data components. Bay-only spots have no meaningful wave data, so their sea-state weight is redistributed proportionally across the rest.

  • Tide movement (30 pts) — bigger daily tidal range and a high/low turn landing near dawn or dusk score higher. Moving water concentrates bait; a dead-flat tide scores lower.
  • Solunar (25 pts) — overlap of the moon's major periods (overhead/underfoot ±1 hr) and minor periods (moonrise/moonset ±30 min) with dawn and dusk, plus a bonus near new and full moon, when spring tides run stronger.
  • Wind (14 pts) — 5–15 mph is the sweet spot: enough to ripple the water without making it unfishable. Dead calm gives up a few points; over 20 mph gives up more.
  • Pressure (10 pts) — measured as a least-squares trend across the whole day in millibars per 24 hours, so a single noisy reading can't swing it. Stable or slowly falling — the classic pre-front bite — scores highest.
  • Rain chance (6 pts) — lower average probability through the day scores higher.
  • Sea state (15 pts, surf/jetty/offshore only) — 1–3 ft with a clean 7-second-plus swell period beats glassy-flat or sloppy and rough.

Some spots also carry local knowledge. Where a spot lists the wind directions it is sheltered from or exposed to, the day's wind is adjusted before scoring. Where it has a preferred tide phase (incoming or outgoing), up to 6 of its 30 tide points reward moving water in that direction at dawn and dusk, and the turn bonus is 6 instead of 12. Spots without this information are scored exactly as described above, and the breakdown says when an adjustment was applied.

Two flags sit outside the score because they are go/no-go calls rather than gradients: FRONT marks a pressure fall steeper than 4 mb/day, and BLOWN OUT marks a day averaging over 22 mph.

This is a planning aid built from real NOAA, Open-Meteo, and astronomical data — not a substitute for checking marine forecasts and small craft advisories before you go out.

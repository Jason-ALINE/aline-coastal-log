DATA SOURCES — Tides: NOAA CO-OPS station predictions (api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov), pulled live and matched to the nearest active station for each spot. Where a subordinate station publishes only high/low times, the curve between them is reconstructed by harmonic interpolation and marked as such. Weather: Open-Meteo forecast API (open-meteo.com). Waves, swell, and sea-surface temperature: Open-Meteo Marine API. Moon phase, moonrise/moonset, and solunar windows are computed in your browser from an abridged lunar theory (the main periodic terms in longitude and latitude), with rise and set refined by interpolation and corrected for horizontal parallax and refraction — expect a few minutes of error, not navigation-grade. All times are US Central, regardless of where you are reading this. The 0–100 fishability score is a heuristic combining all of the above; it is an estimate to help you compare spots and days, not a guarantee. Water temperature is shown for reference and is not part of the score.

How the score works

Each spot gets a 0–100 score. Three questions carry 75 of the points: is the spot protected from today's wind, is the water pushing bait in or pulling it out the way this spot needs, and how hard is the water moving. Every part is judged over the time being scored: the whole selected day, with every hour counted equally, or one hour you pick under Time. No time of day is favoured, and the direction of the water always comes from the tide and current predictions.

  • Wind exposure (25 pts) — wind speed combined with how much open water lies upwind, measured from shoreline maps in 16 directions. Strong wind off the bank: fishable, full points. Strong wind across miles of open bay: choppy and muddy, low points.
  • Bait flow (25 pts) — whether bait is being pushed in (flood, rising water) or pulled out (ebb, falling water). Each spot has a role: passes, jetties, flats and surf want bait pushed in; cove mouths, drains and dropoffs want it pulled out; cuts and structure want any strong flow. Points for the share of the time being scored that it moves the right way. Each row's Bait line shows the times.
  • Current (25 pts) — how hard the water is moving: NOAA predicted current in knots where a station is within 1.5 miles, otherwise how fast the water level changes, compared with that station's usual peak. At bay spots a steady wind can stand in for a weak tide.
  • Solunar (10 pts) — moon major and minor periods falling inside the time being scored, plus a little near new and full moon.
  • Pressure (6 pts) — stable or slowly falling scores highest.
  • Rain chance (4 pts) — lower is better.
  • Slack-water holes (bonus, up to 10 pts) — deep holes, pockets or channels within 300 m, from NOAA depth data, earn points while the water is slack, when fish drop into them. Only where NOAA's depth model agrees with the nautical chart, or no chart soundings exist to check it.
  • Sea state (5 pts, surf/jetty/offshore only) — 1–3 ft with a clean swell. Bay spots skip it and the rest scale up.

Open a spot to see, in plain words, why it scored what it did on wind, bait flow and current.

Two flags sit outside the score because they are go/no-go calls rather than gradients: FRONT marks a pressure fall steeper than 4 mb/day, and BLOWN OUT marks a day averaging over 22 mph.

This is a planning aid built from real NOAA, Open-Meteo, and astronomical data — not a substitute for checking marine forecasts and small craft advisories before you go out.
